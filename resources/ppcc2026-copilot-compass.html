The Copilot Compass

Finding True North Across Copilot, Cowork, Scout, and Agents

Presented by April Dunnam & Bryan Wofford

October 27–29, 2026

Download the slides (.pdf, 3.4 MB) Original PowerPoint (.pptx, 10.2 MB)

In this talk, Bryan and I use four directions to help you choose how to work with Copilot. Start with the job you need done, then decide whether to think it through in Chat, hand it to Cowork, monitor it over time, or build something your team can use.

What's included: the original 42-slide PowerPoint, including presenter notes, a PDF export of the slides, and the guide below. Demo recordings and sample data are not included.

About the product names: the title uses Scout; later slides discuss Autopilot as the cloud-based monitoring experience. Names, preview status, licensing, and billing reflect the 2026 deck. Check current Microsoft documentation and your tenant's availability before planning a rollout.

What's the finish line?

That's the question behind the compass. A quick discussion, a finished deliverable, an ongoing responsibility, and a shared process call for different ways of working.

DirectionUse it whenThe deck's tool
North: SharpenYou're still figuring something out. Research, test a hunch, draft, or analyze while staying involved.Chat, including Researcher, Analyst, and the Office app experiences.
East: MonitorThe work is ongoing. Watch for changes, stalled threads, or follow-ups that need attention.Autopilot, discussed as the successor to Scout.
South: DelegateYou can describe a deliverable and review it when it's done.Copilot Cowork.
West: ScaleA team needs a tool or a process it can reuse.Code, Agent Builder, or Copilot Studio.

The mnemonic in the deck is Now, Eyes on it, Send it off, Whole team. Use it to remember which question each direction answers.

One customer-renewal scenario

The demos follow Maya, a customer-success director who needs a plan for slipping Q4 renewals. Each part of the work has a different finish line.

  1. Test the explanation in Chat. Look for renewal-risk signals across work sources, question the hypothesis, and prepare an analysis for leadership.
  2. Delegate a rescue kit to Cowork. Ask for a tracker, outreach drafts, check-in invitations, and a leadership deck. Review the files and proposed actions.
  3. Watch for accounts going quiet. The monitoring demo starts with a Cowork trigger and introduces the ongoing role described for Autopilot.
  4. Give the team reusable tools. Build a renewal-risk tracker in Code, then prove a Renewal Coach in Agent Builder before adding actions in Copilot Studio.

The useful part is deciding when to hand work over. A discussion can reveal a task worth delegating. A process you've repeated may be ready to turn into a team tool.

Which builder fits?

The session's West direction has three options. Choose based on what you're making and what it needs to do.

BuilderStarting point in the talkWhat to check
CodeA tracker, form, dashboard, or other custom tool.Runtime, publishing, preview status, and who will maintain it. Consider Power Apps when you need a governed business app.
Agent BuilderA first version using instructions and knowledge.Whether answers and knowledge are enough, or the agent needs actions and broader deployment controls.
Copilot StudioAn agent with tools, flows, connectors, MCP, or autonomous triggers.Permissions, versions, testing, staged deployments, analytics, and the channels where people will use it.

The deck uses copy to Copilot Studio as the handoff from a knowledge-based prototype to an agent that needs actions or more lifecycle controls. Confirm the supported path in your environment before relying on it.

Plan the handoffs and approvals

For each handoff, decide what information moves with the task, who reviews the result, and which actions require approval. For ongoing monitoring, define what can run unattended and when it must ask a person.

Check billing before enabling metered experiences. The slides distinguish seat licensing from Copilot Credits and recommend spend policies and caps. Treat the deck's prices and availability labels as conference context, not a current quote.

The talk's “rule of three” is a useful prompt to consider automation after repeating a task. You'll still need to understand exceptions, data access, and support before turning it into an agent.

Try it on a task you already have

Choose one piece of work this week. Write down what done looks like, who needs the result, and whether the work stops or continues. Pick a direction, then identify one point where a handoff would help.

  • Watch the Cowork demos for examples of delegating and reviewing work.
  • Learn what MCP does when your agent needs tools or data.
  • Compare the app-building paths when you're choosing a tool for your team.
  • Work through Agent Academy for hands-on Copilot Studio labs.
