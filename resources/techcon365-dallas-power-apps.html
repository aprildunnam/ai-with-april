Start with three questions

1

Who builds and maintains it?

Consider who will handle the app's future changes and support.

A maker needs to be able to edit the app in maker tools. A developer can work with code, reviews, and debugging. In either case, someone needs to own the app after it's built.

2

Where do users encounter it?

A full-screen app, a page in an existing model-driven app, or a capability inside Copilot?

Don't build a new destination when people need a capability in the place they already work. Match the surface to the task before choosing the authoring tool.

3

How complex and how long-lived?

A quick proof of concept and a long-lived enterprise app have different needs.

Plan for future changes, environments, testing, governance, and support. An app that takes minutes to generate may still take time to maintain.

Configure standard forms and views first. Reach for a generative page, PCF component, or a separate code app only when the scenario needs that additional flexibility.

What still needs your review

The talk covers coding agents and the in-Studio canvas agent, generative pages, PCF, code apps, and prompt-to-app experiences. A canvas app built with AI is still an app someone must review and maintain. You'll need to check the requirements, data model, tests, and governance before people use it.

Keep existing production apps when they meet the need. Extend a model-driven app with a focused page or control rather than rebuilding by default. Check the deck's migration guidance against current product documentation before acting on it.

Check where the app lives

The deck distinguishes Power Platform governance from Copilot Managed Runtime. Different homes can mean different admins, inventories, billing, and editing models. Don't assume an app generated in Copilot can be maintained like a canvas app in Studio.

Before committing, confirm who owns the app, how changes are made, what users need to run it, and which environment and application lifecycle management controls apply.
